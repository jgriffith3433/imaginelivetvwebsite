# The Final Spark: voice

A villain origin told in the dark. You are nobody, you wake in a sewer, and every page is one lit match. The
words are in one of the world's languages; the flashlight shows the English under them. Both layers follow the
same rules, so a translation never adds a sentence the English hasn't got.

## Tone
Cold, close and plain. Dread comes from what is left out. Nothing is explained, nothing is joked about, and the
narrator never says what you feel; you read it off the room.

## Pacing
One page is one beat: one to three short sentences, then one question. Deaths are two or three sentences and the
flat line "You have died." The story speeds up as it goes: the sewer pages breathe, the last pages (shadow,
powers, town) are one act each.

## Choice design
Two choices at a fork, one when the story only moves. Each choice is a thing your hands do, not a label for the
next page. A death must be the plausible mistake, never a trick: the wording of the bad choice is as honest as the
good one.

## Style note: 5 rules
1. **Second person, present tense.** "You hold the flame to the door." Never "you decided", never "you will".
2. **Name one sense and one object.** A smell, a drip, a skeleton's hand. A page without a thing in it is cut.
3. **Light is the clock.** If the page can say what is lit or going dark, it does ("every light goes out").
4. **No narrator opinions.** No "disgusting", "terrible", "seriously ill". Say what is on the floor, not what to feel; the word "foul" is the ceiling.
5. **Choices start with a verb and run five words or fewer.** No bare "Continue": every button says where the hands go ("Go to the gate", "Step into the street").

## Canon the words keep
The matchbox and the rat; an iron gate; a key on a skeleton chained to the wall; the fog and the house; the old man
who sends you away; a white truck; the shadow and its two powers (Consume Light, Shadow Stitch); the town is
destroyed. The old man's spoken line and the talking rat stay in "Waiting for J" (`story/README.md`).
